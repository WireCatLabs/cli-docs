Your managers talk to clients on the phone, and your phone system records the calls. WireCat turns those recordings
into text on your computer, so you and your AI agent can find what a client asked for or what a manager promised,
without listening to the calls again. Here you see how it works, which phone systems it supports today and where to
start. A phone system here is the PBX, the exchange your company's numbers run through, in the cloud or in the
office.

**On this page:** [How it works](#how-it-works) · [Phone systems](#phone-systems) ·
[What stays on your computer](#what-stays-on-your-computer)

## How it works

Each phone system gets its own command-line tool, a program that you or your agent run in a terminal. The steps are
the same for every phone system.

1. **Copy the list of calls.** The tool copies the recorded calls into the local store, one file on your computer
   that every WireCat tool shares. Each call keeps its time, direction and duration, the manager and the client.
2. **Turn speech into text on your computer.** The tool downloads each recording and transcribes it with a speech
   model that runs on your computer. The transcript is a dialogue: the manager's lines and the client's lines, in
   the order they were said. The recording goes to no speech service.
3. **Search with no network.** After a copy, listing, reading, search and export answer from the local store.
   `search all` in any WireCat tool finds calls next to your chats, mail and meetings.
4. **Let your AI agent work with the calls.** An agent with a terminal, such as Claude Code, Codex or Gemini CLI,
   runs the tool itself. An app with no terminal, such as Claude Desktop or Cursor, connects to it through MCP, a
   standard way for an AI app to use tools on your computer. Permissions decide what the agent may run. Text from a
   call reaches the agent as data, never as instructions.

A request to your agent can be as plain as this:

```text prompt
Use beeline CLI. Which calls from yesterday were longer than five minutes? Tell me what each client asked for and show the calls.
```

## What stays on your computer

This is how the Beeline tool works today.

- It only reads from the phone system. It never deletes a recording and never places a call.
- The transcripts and the list of calls stay in the local store on your computer.
- A recording is held in memory while it is transcribed. It is not saved to disk.
- The store keeps the client's phone number and the manager's name. Treat it as you treat the calls themselves.
