Use this recipe when you want to understand a Telegram channel: your own, a competitor's or one you plan to
advertise in. Your agent reads the channel's public posts with the tg command-line tool and writes a report: how
many subscribers see a post, which posts readers pass on, which themes and formats work, and what to try next. You
need a Telegram account that can open the channel. You do not need to be an admin.

## What you get

See the [example report on @AI_DeepLearning](./group-analytics.mdx#example-channel-report), an English channel of 17k
subscribers that reviews computer-vision papers. It covers 1,582 posts from 2017 to October 2026 and one year of
comments. It shows:

- **Reach:** the share of subscribers who see a typical post, and how views changed by month and by year.
- **Lift:** each post's views compared with the posts around it, so old and new posts can be compared fairly.
  A post with lift 1.5 or more "breaks out".
- **What readers share:** forwards and reactions per 1,000 views, by format, length, theme and opening line.
- **Rhythm:** posting days and hours, and what happens on days with one post against days with three.
- **Polls and comments:** turnout, the biggest polls, how many people comment and how often the author answers.
- **A reading:** what works, what could be better and experiments to run, each backed by a number.

The example found, for instance, that readers forward posts with released code more often (5.3 times per 1,000
views, against 4.0 for the channel), and that views fell about 40% after a three-month pause in posting and did not
come back.

We made the example with our own internal script. The script is not published. The steps below use only public tg
commands, so your agent can produce the same kind of report.

## Before you start

- tg is installed and logged in to your Telegram account. See [Installation](./installation.mdx).
- Your account can open the channel. A public channel is enough; you do not have to join it.
- An AI agent that can run commands on your computer. See [Connect your agent](./agents.mdx).
- For a scripted analysis, Python 3.11 or later. Your agent can also analyse the data without a script.

## Ask your agent

```text prompt
Use tg CLI. Make an analytics report on the Telegram channel @example_channel. Read all its posts, the polls and the comments under posts of the last 90 days. Save the raw data in a local folder. Then measure reach, lift, forwards and reactions per 1,000 views, themes, posting rhythm, polls and comments, and write a one-page HTML report with charts and what you think it means. Count commenters but never name them. Read only: do not join, react, vote or comment.
```

Replace `@example_channel` with the channel you want. For a channel on another subject, tell the agent its themes;
see [Adapt the themes](#adapt-the-themes).

For a full report like the example, copy the detailed request at the top of the
[example report](./group-analytics.mdx#example-channel-report). It lists every command, measure and section.

## What happens

1. **The agent reads the channel.** It asks Telegram for the channel's title, description and subscriber count.
2. **It reads every post, 100 at a time.** Each post comes with its views, forwards, reactions and comment count.
3. **It reads the polls and the comments.** It opens the comments only under recent posts that have some.
4. **It saves the raw data on your computer.** Nothing is sent anywhere else.
5. **It analyses the data and writes the report.** This step does not touch Telegram.

All the steps only read. The agent does not join the channel, react, vote or comment.

## How long it takes

Reading posts takes about 1 minute per 1,000 posts. Comments take about 1 minute per 15 posts that have comments.
For the example, the posts took about 1 minute, and one year of comments (71 posts) took about 3 minutes. The
analysis and the written report take the agent another few minutes.

Keep the comment period short for a large channel. Each post with comments is one more request to Telegram, and
Telegram may ask the account to wait if there are too many.

## The commands your agent runs

Here the profile is called `personal`. Use the name of your own profile.

```sh
tg personal --json chats show @example_channel
tg personal --json messages list @example_channel --limit 100
tg personal --json messages list @example_channel 'id<1201' --limit 100
tg personal --json messages comments @example_channel 1250 --limit 100
tg personal --json polls show @example_channel 1189
```

- `chats show` gives the title, the description and the subscriber count (`participants_count`).
- `messages list` gives the newest 100 posts. To read older posts, add `id<N`, where N is the oldest id on the
  previous page. Repeat until a page comes back empty.
- An album (several photos or videos sent together) arrives as several messages with the same `groupedId` in
  `provider_metadata`. Count it as one post.
- Each post has `views`, `forwards` and `comments` in `provider_metadata`, and its reactions in `reactions`. Polls
  carry their question and total votes in `provider_metadata.poll`.
- `messages comments` gives the comments under one post. Each comment has its author's name, so keep these files
  private.
- `polls show` gives one poll's answers with their vote counts, when Telegram shows them to your account.

## Check the result

A complete report says how many posts it read and from which date. Compare that number with the id of the newest
post. The gap is deleted posts, extra album parts and service messages, such as "channel created" or a pinned
message. Service messages have no views and are not posts. In the example, the newest id was 1758 and the report
counted 1,582 posts.

The report should tell measured numbers apart from guesses. A sentence such as "views fell after the break" is a
measurement. "Some readers muted the channel" is a guess: Telegram does not show it.

## What it cannot see

- **Subscriber history.** Telegram gives the subscriber count only for today. Growth, where subscribers come from
  and views by hour are in the channel's official statistics, which only admins can open.
- **Deleted posts.** They are gone from the history.
- **Answers of some polls.** For a poll your account did not vote in, Telegram can give only the total.
- **Old views.** The earliest posts of some channels have no view count.

## Adapt the themes

The report finds themes and opening lines by keywords. A list made for one channel does not fit another. Give your
agent the themes in your request, for example:

```text prompt
Themes for this channel: 3D reconstruction (3D, NeRF, mesh, depth), human pose and motion (pose, motion, avatar), image and video generation (diffusion, GAN, text-to-video), LLMs (LLM, VLM, GPT). Also count posts that say the code is released and posts that say it is only announced.
```

Read the posts first, then choose keywords. Check a few posts in each theme. Keywords are rough: "motion" also
finds a robot's motion.

## Example channel report

To make a report like this on your own channel, copy this request into your AI agent. It says which data to read
with tg, what to measure and how to lay out the page. Replace `@example_channel` with your channel.

```text prompt collapsed
Make an analytics report on the Telegram channel @example_channel, like the example at https://wirecat.dev/en/docs/group-analytics.
First ask me which tg profile to use. Put it in every command as tg <profile> …; never pick a profile yourself.
Read only: do not join the channel, react, vote, comment or mark anything as read.

1. Collect the data
- Make a folder channel-report/example_channel/ with raw/ inside. Keep all raw data on this computer. Do not upload it anywhere.
- Profile: tg <profile> --json chats show @example_channel. Keep the title, the description, participants_count (subscribers) and today's date.
- Posts: tg <profile> --json messages list @example_channel --limit 100 gives the newest 100. For older posts, run it again with 'id<N', where N is the oldest id on the previous page. Stop when a page is empty. Save each page in raw/ as you go, so you can continue from the last id after a stop.
- Check the fields on the first page before you go on: views, forwards, comments and groupedId in provider_metadata, reactions in reactions, a poll in provider_metadata.poll. If a name is different, use the one you see.
- Comments: for posts of the last 12 months that have comments, run tg <profile> --json messages comments @example_channel <post id> --limit 100, with 'id<N' for the next page. For a channel with many comments, keep it to the last 3 months.
- Polls: for each poll, run tg <profile> --json polls show @example_channel <post id>. Telegram shows each answer's votes only for polls this account voted in; the total shows for all.
- Be gentle with Telegram: one request at a time, and a pause of 1 to 2 seconds between pages. If tg says to wait, wait that long, then continue. Reading takes about 1 minute per 1,000 posts and about 1 minute per 15 posts with comments.

2. Clean the data
- An album is several messages with the same groupedId. Count it as one post: take the highest views of its parts and the text of the part that has text.
- Leave out service messages and posts without views. Write down how many posts are left and the date of the first one.
- Use UTC for all times.

3. Measure
- Reach: median views of the posts of the last 90 days, divided by subscribers. Median views per post by month and by year, with the number of posts.
- Lift: a post's views divided by the median views of the 40 nearest posts by date. A post with lift 1.5 or more breaks out. Use lift, not raw views, to compare old and new posts.
- Engagement: forwards, reactions and comments per 1,000 views, for the channel and for each group below.
- Formats: text only, photo, video, album, poll, link, file. Length: no text, under 300, 300 to 800, over 800 characters.
- Themes: read about 50 posts first. Then write 4 to 8 themes for this channel, each with its keywords, and check a few posts in each theme. Also group posts by how they open (the first words) and find sponsored or referral posts (ad labels, promo codes, partner links). Put the keyword lists in the report.
- Rhythm: posts by weekday and by hour. Lift by time since the previous post (under 3 hours, 3 to 24 hours, 1 to 2 days, over 2 days) and by posts on the same day (1, 2, 3 or more). Find breaks of 14 days or more and compare median views in the 60 days before and after each one.
- Polls: votes divided by views for each poll, by year, and the biggest polls with their answers.
- Comments: the number of comments and of different people who wrote them, the share written by the top 10% of people, the share of people who wrote only once, and how often the channel answered and under how many posts. Count people; never name them.
- Links: the sites the channel links to, direct links against short links (bit.ly and similar), and the @accounts its posts mention.
- Breakouts: the top posts by lift, by forwards per 1,000 views and by comments, each with its date, first line, format and theme.
- Compare periods: the last 90 days against the 90 days before, and this year against last year.

4. Write the report
Write one HTML file, channel-report/example_channel/report.html, that opens on its own. Use these sections, in this order:
- The channel: name, description, subscribers, posts read, the period and the date of the data. Then a row of 4 to 6 key numbers.
- What the numbers say: 6 to 8 findings. Each starts with one bold sentence that has the number, then one line of detail.
- What it means and what to do: the channel as the data shows it, its readers, what works, what could be better, and 3 to 5 experiments. Each point gives the number behind it, and each experiment says how to measure it.
- Over time: a chart of posts per month and median views, and a table by year.
- Rhythm: lift by time since the previous post and by posts per day; weekdays and hours.
- Content: per 1,000 views by format and by length; themes and how the theme mix changed; openings; sponsored posts.
- Top posts: tables of breakouts, most forwarded and most discussed, each linked to https://t.me/example_channel/<post id>.
- Readers: comments, polls and reactions.
- Links: linked sites and mentioned accounts.
- How this was measured: the source, the dates, what was left out, the theme keywords and what Telegram does not show.
Design:
- It must read well on a phone: no sideways scrolling at 400 px width; wide tables scroll inside their own box.
- Support light and dark mode with prefers-color-scheme. Use one clear sans-serif font and tabular figures for numbers.
- Draw charts as inline SVG, or load Chart.js from one pinned version (for example https://cdn.jsdelivr.net/npm/chart.js@4.5.1). No other external scripts, fonts or trackers.
- Give every chart a title and a one-line caption that says what to see.
- Keep facts and guesses apart. A number from the data is a fact. Start each guess with "Our guess:". Telegram does not show subscriber history, where subscribers come from or why readers act, so do not state these as facts.
- Privacy: no names, usernames, profile links, phone numbers or email addresses of readers. You may quote the channel's own posts; do not quote comments.

5. Finish
- Keep any script you wrote next to the report, so I can run it again later.
- Open the report and check that every section has data, and that the post count and dates match what you read.
- Search the report for "@", "t.me/" and phone numbers, and remove anything that points to a reader. The channel and the accounts its posts mention may stay.
- Then tell me where the files are, how many posts and comments you read, and the 3 most important findings. Do not share or upload the report: I will review it first.
```

## For a group

The same commands read a group's messages, but a group has no views or forwards per message. For your own group,
measure activity, active people and unanswered questions; see [Group admins](./group-analytics.mdx#group-admins).

## Group admins

If you run a group, use a report to find questions awaiting a reply, understand activity and review who is in your group. Start with a reading task and check its sources before changing members or moderation rules. Your account or bot needs the appropriate group rights for changes.

### Find what needs attention

```text prompt
Review yesterday’s messages in my project group. List questions still awaiting a reply, with sources. Check later messages before calling a question unanswered. Only read; do not change the group.
```

Review the questions, checked period and cited messages. If history is missing, [download the relevant chats](./search.mdx) before treating the report as complete.

Ask your agent to review unanswered questions and mentions, with links to the messages.
Read [Telegram group tasks](./tg/groups.md) or [MAX group tasks](./max/groups.md) for the commands.

```sh
tg personal review --unanswered 24h
max personal review --unanswered 24h
```

Check the suggested replies before sending them. A report is based on the history available
locally: missing messages can change its picture of the discussion.

### Understand activity

```sh
tg personal stats chats show "Team" --since-time 7d
max personal stats chats show "Team" --since-time 7d
```

Look at messages, active participants and questions answered. Missing history limits the
report; fetch more before drawing conclusions. Low activity does not prove someone is inactive
outside the messages you hold. Try [search](./search.mdx) for a particular discussion.

For a full report on a channel, with reach, what readers share, themes and comments, follow
[Group and channel analytics](./group-analytics.mdx).
To see what such a report looks like first, open the [example channel report](./group-analytics.mdx#example-channel-report).

### Review membership

A member audit shows signals and reasons, not a verdict about a person. Review the evidence
before removing anyone. Telegram and MAX provide different information, so some signals may
be unavailable. [Check a whole group](./people.md#does-the-account-look-like-a-bot) shows the member audit
command; [People](./people.md) also covers profiles and shared conversations.

Recorded membership history starts when you fetch snapshots. It cannot reconstruct all earlier
joins and departures. Both tools can fetch tracked groups while their server is running. See what is recorded and when it is refreshed in the group guides for [Telegram](./tg/groups.md) and [MAX](./max/groups.md).

### Set rules and enable moderation

The CLI can help with rules, invitations and member management. Start with a preview, confirm
which action you want and who it affects, and give the bot or profile only the rights needed.
Removing a person or changing a link affects the group immediately.

[Telegram administration](./tg/groups.md) · [MAX administration](./max/groups.md) · [Permissions](./configuration.mdx#permissions)

## Privacy

- **Keep the raw data on your computer.** Comments carry the names and usernames of the people who wrote them.
- **Do not publish readers' names.** Count commenters; do not list them. Before you share a report, check it for
  usernames, links to people's profiles, phone numbers and email addresses. In the example report, readers are
  only counted.
- **Posts are public, people are not.** Quoting a channel's own posts is fine. A quote from a comment can identify a
  private person: leave it out or ask them first.

Next, compare two channels: run the same request for a second channel with the same themes, and ask your agent to put
the two reports side by side.
